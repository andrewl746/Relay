# Hacker’s Handbook

# 👋 Welcome to PivotHacks

Welcome to **PivotHacks** — a 12-hour hackathon built around one idea:

> **Good builders know how to adapt.**
> 

You won’t receive the full challenge at the beginning.

Instead, new information will be revealed throughout the day.

Each reveal may change:

- what you’re building
- who you’re building for
- what matters most
- what constraints you’re working with
- what direction your project should take

The goal is not to perfectly predict what’s coming.

The goal is to **build, adapt, make decisions, and keep moving.**

By the end of the day, we want to see not just what you built — but how your idea evolved.

---

# 🔄 How PivotHacks Works

There are **four Pivots** throughout the day.

## 🎯 Pivot 1 — Your Starting Problem

At 8:00 AM, each team receives its own problem statement.

This is your starting point.

You’ll have two hours to:

- understand the problem
- decide who you want to help
- choose a direction
- start building

This is the only Pivot that is unique to your team.

---

## 🔄 Pivots 2, 3 & 4

The remaining three Pivots are revealed to **every team at the same time**.

Each one introduces new information that may force you to rethink part of your project.

You may need to change:

- your user
- your product direction
- your feature priorities
- your technical approach
- your assumptions
- your scope

A Pivot does **not** automatically mean rebuilding everything.

Sometimes the best response is:

- simplifying
- removing something
- changing how a feature works
- changing your target user
- reframing the product
- keeping your current direction and clearly explaining why

The strongest teams make **intentional decisions**.

---

# 🧠 What Counts as a Good Pivot?

We are not judging you based on how many times you completely change your project.

We care about whether you can answer:

> **What changed, what did that mean for us, and what did we decide to do about it?**
> 

A good Pivot could be:

- changing your entire idea
- changing one major feature
- removing unnecessary functionality
- changing your user flow
- introducing a new stakeholder
- changing your technical implementation
- narrowing your scope

There is no single correct response.

# ❄️ Track 2: Snowflake Challenge

The Snowflake Track is a separate sponsor prize focused on building with Snowflake’s AI capabilities.

Snowflake lets you access industry-leading LLMs through one platform and add AI features to your app through APIs.

You can build things like:

- RAG-powered chatbots
- intelligent search
- AI assistants
- recommendation systems
- document analysis tools
- personalized experiences
- AI-powered features inside a larger app

You’ll have access to a **special 120-day Snowflake student trial** and example resources for using the Snowflake REST API.

# 🎤 Judging Format

Judging begins at **8:15 PM**.

Each team will have approximately **3 minutes**.

## 2 minutes — Pitch + Demo

Your pitch should cover:

1. **What problem did you start with?**
2. **What did you decide to build?**
3. **How did the Pivots change your project?**
4. **What did you end up building?**
5. **Why does it matter?**

If you are competing in the Snowflake Track, also explain:

- where Snowflake is used
- what it powers
- why you chose it

---

## 1 minute — Judge Questions

Be ready for questions like:

- Which Pivot affected your project the most?
- What did you remove or deprioritize?
- What changed from your original idea?
- Why did you choose this technical approach?
- What would you build next with more time?
- What was the hardest decision your team made?
- Where does Snowflake fit into your architecture?

---

# 🎤 Recommended Pitch Structure

You only have two minutes.

Do not spend 90 seconds explaining the problem.

A strong structure:

### 1. Starting Point

“We started with…”

### 2. Biggest Pivot

“Then we learned…”

### 3. Decision

“So we changed…”

### 4. Demo

“This is what we ended up building…”

### 5. Impact

“And this is why it matters.”

Think:

> **We started here → we learned this → so we changed this → and this is where we ended up.**
>
